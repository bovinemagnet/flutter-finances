= Reports and insights
Paul Snow
:revnumber: 0.0.0

Reports present ledger and planning figures as tables with drill-through to the source entries. Insights are explainable observations derived from the same figures. The code is in `packages/finance_core/lib/src/planning` (`report.dart`, `ledger_reports.dart`, `planning_reports.dart`, `date_range.dart`, `insights.dart`, `subscriptions.dart`, `anomalies.dart`). These live in the Dart package; the Java mirror in `server/finance-core` does not include them.

== The report model

A `Report` is a title, a label for the key column, a list of columns, a list of rows, an optional date range or as-of date, and a flag saying whether the figures include local changes that have not yet synchronised (REP 04). A column is either money or a count. Each `ReportRow` carries its amounts or counts and the journal ids behind it, so any figure can be drilled through to its source entries.

Charts and the accessible table beside them render from the same rows. `toTable()` returns a header row followed by one row per entry as display strings, suitable for the accessible table beside a chart.

Ledger reports use the posted ledger (posted and reversed journals plus their reversals) and the same definitions as `LedgerProjection`, so reports, the dashboard and exports agree. Journal ids are listed in effective-date order, with ties in ledger order.

== Date ranges

`DateRange` is an inclusive range of local dates. It offers factories for a calendar year and for the Australian financial year (1 July to 30 June), including the financial year containing a date. The label of a financial year is `FY2026-27`; other ranges are labelled `start..end`. A range can be sliced into calendar months, clipped to its ends. Custom ranges are just two dates.

== Core reports

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Function | Columns | Notes

| `incomeVsExpenses` | Income, Expenses, Net | One row per calendar month of the range. Income postings are negated so earnings are positive.
| `spendingByCategory` | Spending | Net spending per expense category, refunds reducing it, largest first (ties broken by key).
| `spendingByPayee` | Spending | Net spending per merchant, largest first. Entries without a payee share one row labelled `No payee`.
| `accountBalances` | Balance | Each account's balance as a user reads it, all time or as of a date, with liabilities shown as positive amounts owed.
| `netWorthHistory` | Net worth | A point at the end of each calendar month of the range (the range end for a partial last month), using the net-worth definition in xref:domain:net-worth.adoc[Net worth], including the reimbursement receivable.
| `cashFlow` | Amount | For a chosen set of accounts: money in, money out, transfers between the accounts and net. A journal whose postings to the set net to zero is an internal transfer; otherwise its net effect crosses the boundary.
| `receiptCoverage` | Entries (count), Amount | Posted purchases with and without a receipt.
| `budgetVsActual` | Planned, Rollover, Adjustments, Actual, Pending, Remaining | One evaluated budget period, including unbudgeted spending.
| `goalProgressReport` | Target, Allocated, Moved, Reserved, Funding gap | One row per goal; see xref:domain:goals-and-reservations.adoc[Goals and reservations].
|===

Receipt coverage and the missing-receipt insight consider only posted purchases, meaning expense and reimbursable-expense journals that have not been reversed.

The cash-flow report distinguishes transfers within the included account set from flows crossing its boundary, so moving money between two of the chosen accounts never counts as income or spending.

== Insights

`generateInsights` evaluates every rule whose inputs are supplied (REP 03). Insights only describe: they never move money or change budgets, and any follow-up goes through the normal confirmed commands. Each `Insight` has a stable key, the rule, a plain-words trigger, the underlying entry ids, the relevant period and, where relevant, an amount.

[cols="1,3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Fires when | Key

| `overBudget` | A budget line, including unbudgeted spending, has a remaining amount below zero. The amount is the overspend.
| `overBudget:<target>:<period start>`

| `lowCash` | The forecast's lowest balance is below a threshold (zero by default). The entries are the outflow sources up to the lowest day.
| `lowCash:<lowest date>`

| `unreviewed` | There are journals awaiting review; one insight covers the period.
| `unreviewed:<period>`

| `missingReceipt` | A posted purchase of at least a configurable amount has no receipt.
| `missingReceipt:<journal>`

| `possibleDuplicate` | The caller supplies a candidate pair of entries; the pair is sorted so the key is stable.
| `possibleDuplicate:<a>\|<b>`

| `possibleSubscription` | A merchant charges regularly and no schedule covers it yet (see <<_subscription_detection>>). The amount is the latest payment.
| `possibleSubscription:<merchant>:<frequency>:<amount>`

| `unusualTransaction` | A posted purchase is far above what is usual for its merchant or category (see <<_anomaly_detection>>).
| `unusualTransaction:<journal>`

| `spendingSpike` | A category's spending in a month is far above its recent months. The amount is the month's spending.
| `spendingSpike:<category>:<month start>`
|===

The detection rules are fed by `detectSubscriptions`, `detectUnusualTransactions` and `detectSpendingSpikes`, which `AppState.insights` runs for the current budget period (the spike check uses the current calendar month). Candidate duplicates are supplied by the matching code; see xref:domain:matching-and-deduplication.adoc[Matching and deduplication].

=== Subscription detection

`detectSubscriptions` (`subscriptions.dart`, REP 03) looks for merchants that charge regularly. It uses posted expenses with a payee from the last 400 days, grouped by normalised merchant name and then by amount: a payment joins an amount cluster when it is no more than 10% (1000 basis points) above the cluster's smallest amount. A cluster is proposed when it has at least three payments and:

* the median gap between payments falls in a cadence: weekly 6 to 8 days, fortnightly 12 to 16, monthly 26 to 35, quarterly 84 to 98 or annually 358 to 372;
* every gap fits the cadence, or all but one when there are at least three gaps (a skipped month, say);
* the series is still active, meaning the last payment is no more than twice the nominal gap (plus the cadence tolerance) before today;
* no existing schedule has the same normalised payee or name.

Confidence is a heuristic category, never a probability: `high` when every gap fits, every amount is identical and there are at least four payments; `medium` when every gap fits; `low` when one gap does not. Each proposal lists its reasons (same merchant, identical or similar amount, regular or one irregular interval), the payments that form the pattern, and the next due date, which steps on from the last payment (keeping its day of month) until it is on or after today.

The insight offers "Add as bill". `confirmSubscription` then saves the proposal's `toSchedule` as an ordinary expense `RecurringSchedule` (see xref:domain:planning.adoc[Planning and forecasting]), flagged as an estimate when the amounts varied. Nothing is saved until the user chooses that, and once the schedule exists the merchant is no longer proposed. The `confirmed` flag matters to forecasts only for income, so the new bill takes part in the forecast like any expense schedule.

=== Anomaly detection

Both anomaly rules use the same robust test. A value is unusual when its modified z-score exceeds 3.5, computed in exact integer arithmetic as `6745 x (amount - median) > 35000 x MAD`. MAD, the median absolute deviation, is raised to at least a tenth of the median and one minor unit, and an optional minimum excess over the median can also be required.

* `detectUnusualTransactions` compares each posted purchase in the period with earlier purchases from the same merchant in the last 365 days when there are at least five, otherwise with earlier purchases in the same main expense category (the one with the largest expense posting) when there are at least five. With less history the purchase is not judged. The insight shows the amount, the usual median and how many earlier purchases were compared.
* `detectSpendingSpikes` compares each expense category's net spending in a calendar month (refunds reduce it) with its monthly spending over the previous six months, limited to months since the first posted entry. With fewer than three history months nothing is reported. A category whose history months were all empty is marked as first spending and described as spending after that many months with none.

Anomalies only explain. They never change a budget, recategorise an entry or move money, and like other insights they can be viewed, dismissed, snoozed or switched off.

=== Dismiss, snooze and disable

`visibleInsights` filters the generated list for a given day:

* an `InsightState` records a dismissal, or a `snoozedUntil` date before which the insight is hidden and from which it shows again;
* an `InsightRuleSetting` disables a rule entirely and can hold the rule's threshold (the missing-receipt amount or the low-cash balance).

Because keys are stable, a dismissal or snooze stays attached to the same condition, for example one category in one budget period.
